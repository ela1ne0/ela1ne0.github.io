Nebraska Flood Outreach

In collaboration with the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, I spearheaded and designed a multi-channel communication system to translate complex GIS flood-risk data into accessible, human-centered visuals for low-income rural populations. By bridging the gap between deep learning analytics and user-facing design, I helped create a framework adopted by U.S. HUD-related stakeholders to assist in disaster recovery patterns.

Role

Product Design Graphic Design Research Intern

Team

Just me! (with faculty research collaboration)

Timeline

3 Months (to design visuals)

Tools

Figma · Procreate · Adobe Illustrator · ArcGIS · GPT-4 · DALL·E

The Challenge

Flood data is typically presented through high-density GIS visualizations that assume technical literacy. For many rural residents (particularly those with limited digital access) these maps can feel overwhelming or inaccessible.

The challenge was twofold:

    1. Translate complex geospatial flood data into clear, human-centered visuals.

    (The visuals would later be distributed in the form of post cards)

    2. Ensure the system met public-sector accessibility and compliance standards.

This project required balancing scientific accuracy with emotional sensitivity. The information was urgent — but it couldn’t feel alarming.

Research Foundation

Alongside the design work, I studied flood recovery patterns using GIS and deep learning models (Siamese U-Net), contributing to a peer-reviewed publication in Environment and Planning B: Urban Analytics and City Science (2026).

The research informed:

  • How recovery trajectories differ across communities
  • Which spatial indicators most strongly predicted long-term displacement
  • How to visually represent recovery patterns without oversimplifying risk
  • Rather than treating the interface as a static map, I approached it as a narrative translation layer between machine learning outputs and human decision-making.

Strategy & Execution

  • Print-First Accessibility Recognizing limited broadband access in rural regions, I designed postcard-style outreach materials that mirrored the web interface — ensuring consistency across print and digital.
  • AI-Assisted Iteration To accelerate time-sensitive disaster communication, I incorporated AI tools (GPT-4, DALL·E) into early design drafts while maintaining human oversight for compliance and clarity. This reduced iteration time by approximately 30%.

The Impact

  • 8,000+ Residents Reached: Distributed flood-risk outreach materials across 59 Nebraska Communities
  • Adopted by HUD-Related Stakeholders: The communication framework informed disaster recovery support initiatives.
  • Peer-Reviewed Research Contribution: Check it out here
  • 30% Faster Iteration: AI-assisted drafting accelerated disaster-response design cycles.
